# Describing Your Product Surface for AI

> AI invents whatever you leave out. This lesson builds the one habit that fixes most disappointing AI output: describing the thing you're working on — a screen, a service, a report — in plain words, completely enough that a stranger could picture it.

## Start Here: The Stranger Test (5 minutes)

Pick one screen or one service you know well. Now describe it — out loud or in writing — to an imaginary new coworker who has never seen it. Could they sketch it from your words alone?

Most first attempts sound like this: "It's the invoices page." A description that passes the stranger test sounds like this: "The screen has a header with the customer's name, a table of invoices with dates and amounts, and a side panel showing payment status."

That second sentence is the whole skill. Nothing more advanced is required in this lesson.

## Why This Works

When you ask an AI for something and leave gaps, it doesn't ask you to fill them — it fills them itself, with plausible-sounding fiction. The invoice table gets columns your product doesn't have. The service gets features nobody built. This isn't the tool being bad; it's the tool doing exactly what a stranger would do if forced to guess.

Complete plain-language description removes the guessing. That's it. No special formats, no technical vocabulary — just the discipline of saying what's actually there.

## Describing Screens in Plain Words

Walk the surface like a tour guide: what areas the screen has (top, middle, side), what sits in each area, what information is shown, and what the screen looks like when things go wrong — when it's still loading, when there's nothing to show, when something failed.

## Describing Services in Plain Words

Same idea, no visuals: what the service does in one sentence, who or what uses it, what it needs to do its job, and what must never happen ("it must never send an invoice twice").

## Where This Goes Next

Later lessons will give you named templates for these descriptions, and eventually formats precise enough for a computer to check. Every one of them is just a sharper version of the sentences you wrote today. If you can pass the stranger test, you're ready for all of it.

## Assignment

Choose one screen you own (or one service, if that's your world). Write a plain-language description that passes the stranger test: someone who has never seen your product could sketch or summarize it from your words alone.

### Deliverables

- A markdown document (my-screen.md or my-service.md)
- At least 3 areas of the screen, or 3 jobs of the service, each described in complete sentences
- One "when it goes wrong" moment explicitly described (empty, loading, failed, wrong input)
- One rule the thing must always follow, in plain words
