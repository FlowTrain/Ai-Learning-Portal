# The First Win: Get 20 Minutes Back

> This is not a lesson about AI. It's a lesson about a chore you already have to do this week — done in a third of the time. No vocabulary, no theory, no setup. One task, one rule, one saved file.

## Pick the Chore (2 minutes)

Choose one small, dull task that's already on your plate this week. Good candidates: summarizing a long document you have to read anyway, turning messy meeting notes into a tidy email, drafting a first version of something you'd normally stare at a blank page for. Pick something low-stakes — nothing confidential, nothing where a mistake would embarrass you.

## Do It (10 minutes)

Open whichever AI chat tool your organization provides. Talk to it the way you'd hand the task to a helpful colleague — in ordinary sentences. Tell it three things: what you're trying to produce, who it's for, and paste in the raw material you're working from. If the first answer isn't right, say what's wrong with it the way you would to a person ("shorter," "less formal," "you missed the part about the deadline") and let it try again.

That's the entire technique for today. Plain sentences, honest corrections.

## The One Rule

**Nothing the AI writes leaves your hands unchecked.** Read the output the way you'd read a colleague's draft before forwarding it — you're still the author, it's still your name on it. (And the flip side of the same rule: don't hand it anything you couldn't hand a stranger — no confidential data.)

This one rule is what makes everything else in this program safe to try.

## Notice What Just Happened (3 minutes)

Compare: how long does this chore usually take you? How long did it take today, including the corrections? For most people, on the right chore, the answer is "about a third of the time." That feeling — "this saved me 20 minutes" — is the entire point of this lab. Not mastery. Just one real win on one real task.

## Assignment

Do the chore for real — the output should be something you actually use this week, not an exercise.

### Deliverables

- The finished chore itself (the summary, the email, the draft — whatever you made)
- first-win.md with three sentences: what the task was, roughly how much time it saved, and one thing you'd say differently to the AI next time
