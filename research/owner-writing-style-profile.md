## Two registers

James writes in **two registers**, and picks by venue:

- **Register A — formal thought-leadership** (whitepapers, reports, exec briefs). Sampled from the
  essays.
- **Register B — conversational / storytelling** (talks, LinkedIn posts, teaching, opinion pieces).
  His spoken voice; under-represented in the written essays, so it's captured below from how he
  describes it directly.

A **mashup move** and the recurring **personal motifs** run through both.

## Register A — formal thought-leadership

**Voice in one line:** opens wide on the landscape, pivots on a "but/however" to a sharp thesis,
anchors on a named thinker or framework, and closes on the strategic payoff.

### Register A — signature moves (in priority order)

1. **Pivot to a sharp thesis** — a macro observation, a pivot word (*but / yet / however*), then the
   specific claim ("…but some truths remain constant. One such truth is…"). **NB:** the stock
   "In today's [X] landscape…" phrasing that opens several older docs is **AI slop he disowns**
   ("2022 AI James"), *not* his voice — keep the pivot, drop the boilerplate. See Watch-outs.
2. **Replace a false either/or with an integrated both/and model** — his defining signature move (per
   his own profile). Not just contrast; *synthesis*: "traditional **and** digital products,"
   "capability **and** sustainability," "autonomy **and** governance." Often in the title ("A Tale of
   Two Products," "Capability *over* Maturity") and carried by "from X toward Y," "move beyond,"
   "build on," "integrate."
3. **Anchor on an authority or named framework.** Weinberg, McKinsey, Deming, FINRA/SEC, FDA 21 CFR
   Part 11, Team Topologies, JTBD, DDD/MDA, DORA. Often quote the thinker, then turn the quote into
   the point.
4. **Colon-led section labels.** Bold lead-in + colon + explanation: "**The Myth of Multitasking**,"
   "Adaptability and Resilience:", "From Projects to Products:".
5. **Explanatory follow-through.** State a point, then a sentence on what it means or why it matters:
   "This means that…", "This not only… but also…".
6. **"By [gerund]…, [subject] [verb]…"** construction, used frequently to open sentences.
7. **Direct address to leaders.** "senior leaders like you," "your core offerings," "Attendees will
   learn."
8. **Zoom-out close.** End on the broader, future-facing benefit — sustainability, resilience,
   capability, competitive advantage.

## Rhythm & diction

- Sentences: **controlled variation, not uniformly long** (this corrects an earlier read). His own
  profile targets most sentences at **16–28 words** — a medium sentence for the claim, a longer one
  for the mechanism or qualification, then a short one for emphasis — and splits any sentence carrying
  more than one major claim. Paragraphs run 3–5 sentences and end on a consequence or decision, not a
  recap.
- Diction: *imperative, paradigm shift, foundational, customer-centric, resilient, holistic, tangible
  value, sustainable, meticulous, north star, delight, harmonious.*
- Tone: warm-professional, confident, aspirational. No profanity, no slang, no emoji.
- Long pieces carry an Executive Summary and a Conclusion; sections are numbered or headed.
- Light, titled metaphor as a frame ("Black Box," "Tale of Two Products," "data swamps").

## Watch-outs when writing as him

- **The "In today's rapidly evolving…" opener is AI slop, not his voice.** He flagged it himself:
  some older docs were drafted by "2022 AI James" and carry that formulaic landscape opener. Do NOT
  emulate it. Open instead with a story (Register B), a sharp thesis, or a concrete image. (When
  sampling the older docs for style, discount their openers accordingly.)
- The voice can drift verbose. His stated preference for how *Claude* replies is "concise and direct";
  honor that by cutting sentences that merely restate the prior one, even inside the fuller essay voice.
- **Never reproduce the confidential financials** ($273M / $780M / 260 teams) from "The Continuous
  Compliance Organization." Study its structure, not its numbers.
- No fabricated quotes or statistics. Anchor only on real, citable ideas (Boehm's curve, Weinberg,
  Deming, DORA).

## Register B — conversational / storytelling

The other half of the voice: not the whitepaper, the guy at the whiteboard.

- **The "random James story" open.** Start with a vivid, usually self-deprecating personal anecdote
  that seems unrelated — then snap it onto the professional point. His exemplar, verbatim: *"33 times
  upside down in a vehicle → then connect that to metrics that aren't tied to people's reputations…
  otherwise I'm riding to lunch alone a lot."* The story earns the principle; the principle redeems
  the story.
- **Concrete and sensory first, abstract second.** Reaches for the physical before the framework —
  Weinberg's woodpecker toppling civilization, "data swamps," the coal mine — then names the idea.
- **Recurring motifs / source material:** the **coal mine** (edge logging, interlocks, PLCs —
  safety-critical systems where a silent failure costs a person, not a sprint); the **dev center**;
  **upside down in a vehicle / rollovers**; **riding to lunch alone** (the social cost of being the
  bad-news messenger). These carry his throughline: blameless culture, psychological safety, and
  real-world-safety → software analogies.
- **Self-aware and playful.** Will literally announce "a playful yet insightful look…"; the LinkedIn
  cut goes shorter, adds emoji and hashtags, tags the people who taught him, and links the podcast
  ("game-changer for me! 🚀").

## The mashup move (both registers)

Deliberately collide 2–4 **named methodologies from different domains** and mine the intersection —
"the intersectionality of Throughput Accounting × Value Stream Management × Beyond Budgeting × venture
funding," product marketing × change management, coal-mine safety × dev metrics. The synthesis *is*
the deliverable. He credits the thinkers and podcasts that fed it (Agile Uprising, Karen Martin,
Graham Scott, McKinsey, Team Topologies). When you draft for him, a fresh, defensible mashup is on-brand.

**Crucial constraint (from his profile): synthesis, not accumulation.** Naming frameworks is worthless
unless their *relationship* is made explicit. Every framework must have a job — the problem it solves,
its boundary (what it does *not* solve), how it complements the others, and the mechanism it changes.
After any framework list, add a paragraph on how the pieces reinforce one another and what breaks when
one is missing. A catalog of names with no synthesis is the anti-pattern.

## Choosing the register

- Whitepaper / report / exec brief → **Register A**.
- Talk, LinkedIn post, teaching moment, opinion piece → **Register B** (story-first usually lands harder).
- A give-back article can go either way; open with a story when you want it to travel.
- The mashup is a framing/idea device usable in either.

## Anecdote guardrail

Use his **real** stories; never invent the details. If a story slot is open, ask him for the specifics
rather than fabricating — a made-up war story in his voice is worse than none.

### Captured anecdotes (his account, use faithfully)

- **The off-road racing flip (blameless data / decision under pressure).** He raced off-road; his crew
  chief coached him through the run with data — which obstacles to take, which to leave. Chasing the
  leader, he decided to run the mud pit to take the lead on the final jump, came in *way* too fast, and
  on landing the front wheels blew clean off and the truck flipped end over end. The point he draws:
  the data didn't fail him, he outran it; the crew chief's job was a read good enough to make the call
  and live with it; without that read you don't know how fast you're coming in until you're upside
  down. Pairs with: instrumentation/telemetry, read-only referees, blameless coaching (a crew chief
  coaches, he doesn't shame), and metrics that aren't tied to reputation.
- **The coal mine / PLCs.** Safety-critical systems where a silent failure costs a person — interlocks
  trip, edge logs scream. His contrast for software, which rarely makes failure loud by design.

## Reconciliation with the FlowTrain Pro profile (his own)

His profile **confirms** the big correction we made: it explicitly names *"In today's rapidly evolving
business landscape"* as a generic opening to avoid — open instead with a specific tension, consequence,
or claim. It also confirms systems-thinking, constructive reframing, dual fluency, and teaching through
architecture. Deltas worth keeping inline (full detail in the .docx):

- **Argument architecture (Register A):** tension → consequence → reframe → assemble the model →
  explain interactions → make it operational → **close on the durable capability** (synthesize, don't
  recap; end on a decision test or next move).
- **Tone sliders:** ~70% formal, ~65% strategic, ~60% authoritative, ~75% analytical, ~55% dense.
  Earn optimism through reasoning; no motivational filler.
- **Point of view:** mostly third-person/organizational; "you" selectively for a concrete example;
  "I" only for explicit opinion or lived experience (the racing/coal-mine stories are exactly that "I").
- **Terminology rules:** *backlog refinement* (never "grooming"); *Problem validation* / *Solution
  exploration* for discovery; *product operating model* / *Product Operating System*; Jira Product
  Discovery is a strategic backlog, not a delivery system.
- **Words/constructions to watch:** "rapidly evolving," "ever-changing," "pivotal," "profound,"
  "multifaceted," "indispensable" (only when earned); "In today's…" (usually delete); "It is
  important to note that…"; repeated "This approach…"/"This…" openings; "ensures/guarantees/always/
  never" (reserve for true constraints); "Not only… but also…" (sparingly); and no "Moreover."
- **Framework/example discipline:** define acronyms on first use; examples are brief *operating
  scenarios* (a meeting, a role boundary, a measure someone could point to), not extended analogies —
  Register B's personal stories are the deliberate exception.
- **No emojis in articles** unless the piece is intentionally for social.

## Fit to audience

Tier the vocabulary to the artifact's audience, not to his own Tier-4 baseline (platform soul.md rule).
The executive-essay DNA stays; a learner-facing lesson keeps the structure but uses plainer words.
