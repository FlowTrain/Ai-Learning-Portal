# Pharma Ecom Greenfield + Q2 Release — Founder-Market-Fit (captured 2026-07-11)

Surfaced in the StructureView branding chat while working out the Q2 Release product. Too

## The story (career-defining credential)

**2016. Greenfield pharma e-commerce platform, shipped in 11 months. 25 teams across 4
continents and 4 time zones. Every 6 weeks: a release to a closed beta of ~200 pharmacies.**
Coordination was done with **hand-drawn flip charts and people physically running around** to
see who was green to go.

Why it matters:
- **Founder-market-fit, textbook.** The person building Q2 Release has *personally run* the exact
  problem Q2 Release solves — multi-team, multi-geo, closed-beta, gated release coordination — at
  serious scale, under real regulatory stakes (pharma), and won (11 months greenfield is fast).
- **The pain is lived, not researched.** Flip charts and runners for 25 teams is the visceral,
  narratable origin of the product. That's book-chapter and sales-deck gold: "I coordinated a
  25-team, 4-continent pharma release with flip charts. Never again."
- **The vision scales the memory:** "now imagine seeing **86 teams** do this — each team,
  location, and domain **greening up to go** — nothing on the market does this that I've seen."
  That's the Q2 Release product thesis in one sentence, grounded in having done the manual version.

## Q2 Release — the product model (from the same chat)

- **Rail role:** dispatch. "A release is literally a dispatch event — sending something down the
  track." Structure → Quality → **Ship**.
- **Two seats in the cab (the PRR Q2 needed a two-person crew — engineer + fireman):**
  - **Engineer = the developer.** Sets the route, the PR stack, the flag logic, the population
    splits. Controls speed, start, stop.
  - **Fireman = the business person.** Maintains the pressure — rollout %, cohort health, budget/
    burn, go/no-go sign-off. Can't drive, but the train doesn't move without them.
  - *"Neither can run a Q2 alone. That's not a limitation — that's the point. It's a collaboration
    tool disguised as a release tool."* Hook: **"Two seats in the cab. One train to ship."**
- **What it does:** multi-PR stacked releases, A/B testing, population routing, feature-flag
  management — **without being in the code.** The business half participates in the release
  without touching the toolchain. Closed-beta orchestration is the entry use case.
- **Steam-pressure gauge = rollout %** (0 → full steam as the population reaches 100%). Iconic UI.
- **The compounding play:** the domain setup (a simple web form / wizard) + real release cycles
  produce **organizational-intelligence patterns** — dependency risk, bottleneck detection,
  release-readiness signatures — visible only because the platform instruments the coordination
  nobody else instruments. This is the moat the V5 brief calls "organisational intelligence."

## The domain model is the real work (owner's own point)

"That changes the architecture — the importance of building out the domain model and capturing
the dependencies." Q2 Release only works if the **domain/team/dependency graph** is captured
first (Team Topologies: stream-aligned / complicated-subsystem / platform / enabling). This is
the same **Dependency Graph** from the middle-tier architecture — Q2 Release *consumes* it. The
"Tale of Two Products" framing: the sold product + the digital products used to service and
support it, carved into domains and teams. Q2 Release makes the second product's release motion
visible.
